### Investigation of the Fluent Design System

The Fluent Design System, particularly as applied to a Windows 11 application, is characterised by a tranquil two-layer surface where the chrome recedes, allowing content to take precedence. The colour palette is minimal, and the materials utilised are authentic. Scriptorium already aligns with these principles. However, the anticipated restyling elements (Mica, Acrylic, Reveal) are predominantly unavailable within CustomTkinter, and attempts to replicate them may result in an inferior appearance compared to the existing solid dark utility.

This investigation has been documented in notes/01_Active/notes_033.txt, and no alterations have been made to the application.

### Current Understanding of Fluent Design

Fluent Design encompasses three distinct components:

1. **Fluent Design (Neon)**: Initiated in 2017 with Windows 10, it includes elements such as Light, Depth, Motion, Material, and Scale, prominently featuring Acrylic and the now-retired Reveal highlight.

2. **Windows 11 Signature Fluent**: This represents the current design framework, characterised by a seamless, calm, personal, familiar, and coherent aesthetic. This forms the relevant contractual framework for design.

3. **Fluent 2**: This includes cross-platform kits featuring tokens (global hex values and semantic aliases), a 4px spacing ramp, and a radius scale. It is intended for WinUI and React, rather than being applicable as a Tk theme.

The guiding principles for Windows 11 relevant to this context include a softer and more uncluttered aesthetic, with colour employed solely to denote hierarchy, and an emphasis on maintaining a familiar user experience without introducing a new learning curve.

### Signature Elements of Fluent Design

- **Geometry**: Microsoft specifies an 8px margin for windows, dialogs, and flyouts; 4px for in-page controls; and 0px when a window is snapped or maximised.

- **Layering**: The design features two distinct layers rather than a stack of cards: the base chrome (toolbar, action bar) and a content layer (the two panes). Dialogues are positioned above with a 1px stroke.

- **Materials (Fluent 2 / WinUI)**:
  - **Solid**: Predominantly utilised in the UI, including this application.
  - **Mica**: An opaque material with a wallpaper tint for long-lived windows, turning neutral when unfocused.
  - **Acrylic**: A frosted glass effect, employed solely for transient menus and flyouts.
  - **Smoke**: A translucent black material used beneath modals.

- **Typography**: Utilising Segoe UI Variable with sizes of caption 12, body 14, and subtitle 16 semibold. While Tk can only request Segoe UI, the panes set at 13px are sufficiently close. Italics should not be used for emphasis.

- **Colour**: One accent colour (Microsoft blue ~#0078D4) is employed, with surfaces near #202020 / #2C2C2C; hairlines are used instead of drop shadows. The accent colour should only be applied to primary actions and selections.

### Existing Strengths of Scriptorium

The application effectively functions as a Windows 11 utility, featuring a dark and unobtrusive interface with a dual-pane content layer. The blue primary action labelled 'Process', the single green commit labelled 'Accept', and the remaining elements presented in grey30 and Segoe contribute to a calm and familiar experience. It is advised against restyling this into the WinUI Gallery.

### Limitations of CustomTkinter

Mica and Acrylic require DWM system backdrops and transparent clients, whereas CustomTkinter (CTk) renders opaque frames. Third-party theme engines, such as win32mica or pywinstyles, are prohibited in this project. Additionally, extending the DWM frame into the client area (DwmExtendFrameIntoClientArea -1) may lead to visual disturbances, such as white flashes or holes in CTk widgets.

Consequently, it is advisable to utilise honest solid layers rather than attempting to create a faux blur effect. This approach aligns with Fluent 2's guidelines, particularly when the toolkit cannot support the intended material.

Adhering to the operating system’s accent and light/dark theme aligns with Fluent’s Personal principle. Scriptorium is intentionally confined to a dark theme with hard-coded hex values. The introduction of a light mode should not be considered within the scope of Fluent-related tasks.
